The Board shows on one screen who is waiting for you across every project, and the mascot can leave the app to tell you while you are somewhere else. The editor gained suggestions, documentation and errors from the project itself, with WebStorm's or VS Code's shortcuts. And when the Claude account hits its limit, the session waits and carries on by itself when the window renews, with the app open.

### The Board replaces the Studio

![The Board with every project's sessions in four columns: the A key opens the one that has waited longest, and ⌘⇧O comes back to the Board.](/changelog/0.10.0/quadro.mp4)

<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>O</kbd>, or the button beside the tabs, opens the agent sessions of every project, or only of the current one, in four columns: **Needs you**, **Working**, **Answered** and **Error**. Each card says what the session wants (the command waiting for permission, the question, the end of the answer or the error), how long it has been waiting and whether you have seen it. The <kbd>A</kbd> key opens the one that has waited longest.

### The mascot outside the app

![The mascot standing on top of a window, walking along it.](/changelog/0.10.0/mascote.mp4)

On macOS, in **Settings › Appearance › Mascot outside the app** (off by default), the mascot stays on top of everything, standing on the front window: it rides along when the window moves, falls when it goes away and has a life of its own when nothing is happening. When a session needs you or ends a turn with Persisty in the background, it rises and tells you, and clicking what it says opens the session. A click on it opens a balloon with who is waiting, for how long and what they asked for, with shortcuts to the session and to the Board, and **Do not disturb**.

- Clicking or dragging the mascot does not take the focus away from the app you are in.
- To keep it only where you put it, turn off **The mascot walks on your windows** in **Settings › Appearance**.

### An editor that understands code

- **Code intelligence** in TypeScript, JavaScript and Rust: suggestions come from the project, with each one's type and documentation, and picking a function from another file writes the `import` for you. Resting the pointer on a name shows its documentation, opening the `(` of a call shows its parameters, and errors are underlined. In Rust, `cargo check`'s errors refresh every time you save.
- When the language server is not on the computer, the app downloads it. The status bar shows its state (downloading, indexing, ready or stopped) and how many errors and warnings the file has; clicking there restarts the server or shows its log.
- **Go to definition**, into other files too. A definition in the standard library or in a dependency outside the project opens read-only, with a lock on the tab: you can browse and read, but nothing is edited. In Rust, the standard library needs `rust-src` in your toolchain (`rustup component add rust-src`); the app never changes the toolchain on its own.

### WebStorm or VS Code shortcuts

- **Settings › Editor › Keymap**: WebStorm (default), WebStorm (macOS) or VS Code. Each key does the real action; where the action does not apply, the key goes on to the app.
- In WebStorm: <kbd>⌘</kbd> + click or <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> go to the definition, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> shows the parameters, <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> opens the documentation, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⌥</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⌥</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> go back and forward through the places you visited, <kbd>F2</kbd> goes to the next error and <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>N</kbd> opens a file by name. On Windows and Linux, the same actions use <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>.
- **Editor tabs**: <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>T</kbd> reopens the last closed tab, and <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd> opens the tab switcher, in the order you used them, with the focus in the terminal too.
- The **command palette** (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd>) also lists the editor's actions, with the chosen keymap's keys, and **Find action** (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> in WebStorm, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> in VS Code) opens the palette with only those.
- **Settings › Shortcuts** lists every key of the keymap and of Persisty, spelled out and searchable. When a key does one thing in the code and another outside it, the row says both.

Some keys changed since 0.9.4:

- In WebStorm, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> duplicates the line (the next occurrence moved to <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>G</kbd>), <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>G</kbd> goes to a line, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>[</kbd> and <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>]</kbd> go to the start and end of the block, and <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>↑</kbd> and <kbd>↓</kbd> move the whole statement, with the comments just above it. The VS Code keymap keeps most of the old keys.
- <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>W</kbd> no longer closes the window: in the Code panel, outside the text, it closes the editor tab; in the text, the chosen keymap's key applies; anywhere else in the app it does nothing.
- In a session's code, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> take the cursor to the start and end of the line; outside the code, they still switch sessions.
- On the Mac, the app's shortcuts answer to <kbd>⌘</kbd> only: <kbd>⌃</kbd> + a letter no longer opens the project bar or search, and <kbd>⌃</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> does not save the file. On Windows and Linux it stays <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>.

### Claude accounts and quota

- **Out of quota, the session waits**: when the account hits its limit and no other has room, the session header shows "Out of quota · back at 19:14 on …" and the request queue waits, in every session on that account. **Let the queue go now** lets the requests through.
- **Carries on by itself when the window renews**: with the app open, Persisty types "continue" once in the conversation the limit cut short, and the account's queues move again. If the time passed with the app closed, the resumption comes as soon as it opens. A notice tells you when the account runs out of quota and when each session resumes; if you type first, the resumption is yours. The option is **Go on with the task the limit cut**, in **Settings › AI accounts**.
- **Switch a session's account from its footer** (Pro): click the account's e-mail to see the connected accounts, each with its fuller quota window. While the agent is answering, the switch happens when the answer ends. The same e-mail in two organisations shows as two accounts, with the organisation's name beside it.
- **A project pinned to an account** (Pro), the company's for example: its sessions only open on that account, never switch and, at the limit, wait for its window to renew. It is in the project's settings, under **Claude account**.
- **An account reserved for the projects pinned to it** (Pro), in **Settings › AI accounts**: rotation and the other projects leave that account alone, and the footer says why.
- Every account now uses the same folder for conversations and memory, so `/resume` on any account finds the whole conversation. On the first launch Persisty merges what each account kept apart, deleting nothing: whatever differed goes to `claude-profiles/backup-conversas-<date>`, listed in `LEIA-ME.txt`.

### A new request queue

- The queue now works like a chat: the header says what the agent is doing and has **Pause queue**; the next requests are numbered, with when each one goes and why the ones below are waiting. You can drag to reorder, edit a request that has not gone yet, send one next and take one out.
- The text box takes images (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>V</kbd>, drag or the paperclip; up to 8 per request, 10 MB each). They stay on this computer, and the agent gets their paths at the end of the request.
- On the free plan, pausing, reordering, sending next and taking out work too.

### Session tools, files and terminal

- The session's tools sit in a thin bar on its right edge: Code, Search and Notes, then Changes, Restore points and Queue, and Details. Even with the panel closed, Changes shows how many files changed and the Queue how many requests are waiting.
- **MCP servers** in **Settings › MCP servers**: a selection of 16 official servers (GitHub, Linear, Atlassian, Notion, Supabase, Figma, Sentry, Stripe and others), search in the official MCP registry and a test before installing. Each project turns on its own in **Project settings › MCP servers**, none on by default. Tokens stay in the Keychain, and only Claude Code gets the servers.
- The Files list changes width, and right-clicking a file or folder has **Show in Finder** (File Explorer on Windows, the file manager on Linux).
- Images open in the editor (PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP, BMP and ICO), with their dimensions and size. An SVG gets a preview, like Markdown, drawn as an image and running no script.
- In the terminal, paths that exist on disk become links (including `main.ts:12:5` in an error message), and addresses starting with `www.` too. Open them with <kbd>⌘</kbd> + click (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + click on Windows and Linux).
- Pro and Team are subscribed on the website, and the plan reaches the app by itself. On the free plan, **Settings › Account** has **See plans**.

### Fixed

- Switching accounts no longer interrupts what the session left running in the background: it waits for a subagent or a command to finish before reopening Claude on the other account.
- "When the 5-hour window renews" now uses the time of the session's own account, not the computer's.
- Queued requests and the automatic "continue" were sometimes left as a draft in Claude Code, typed but not sent.
- A very long queued request (from about 16 thousand characters) never went out and held the ones below, and a `;` at its end disappeared.
- **AI accounts** warns when two connected accounts signed in with the same e-mail.
- Sticky scroll no longer shows a block when the line just below already closes it.
